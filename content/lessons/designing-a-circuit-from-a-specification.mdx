Design starts before you place a gate. Translate the request into a contract:

1. Name every input and output.
2. Write the rule in plain language.
3. Express the rule with Boolean operations.
4. Make a truth table for every possible input combination.
5. Build the circuit and compare its output with the table.

Here is a small specification: **turn on the output when at least two of three sensors are on.** A Boolean expression for that rule is:

```text
Q = (A AND B) OR (A AND C) OR (B AND C)
```

Each AND term identifies one pair of sensors that is on. The OR combines those possible pairs. This is a complete design at the level of gates; it does not require us to guess what the sensors represent.

You receive the inputs, output, and behavior; your job is to turn the specification into a working NAND-only circuit. The [binary addition quiz](/puzzles/binary-addition?lesson=designing-a-circuit-from-a-specification&course=intro-to-computer-engineering) is an optional bridge to the next course: arithmetic is also a circuit specification built from Boolean operations.

## Where to go next

You now have the vocabulary to study adders, multiplexers, registers, memory, and eventually a CPU datapath. Those topics are natural follow-ups, but the core habit remains the same: define inputs and outputs, write the rule, and test every case you can name.
