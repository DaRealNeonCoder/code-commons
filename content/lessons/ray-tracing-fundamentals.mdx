# Ray tracing is a different loop

Ray marching asks how far it may safely travel. Ray tracing solves an
intersection equation, such as the quadratic for a ray and sphere. For each
pixel:

1. Construct a primary ray from the camera through the pixel.
2. Find the closest positive intersection.
3. Compute the hit normal and direct lighting.
4. Reflect the ray and recurse or iterate for another bounce.

One reflection step already demonstrates the central idea:

```glsl
vec3 reflectedRay = reflect(rayDirection, normal);
```

In codeloop's fragment runtime, a small fixed loop and a few analytic shapes
are practical. Full global illumination, acceleration structures, meshes, and
many-bounce path tracing are outside the runtime and outside this course.

## Checkpoint

The [ray intersection puzzle](/shaders/graphics-ray-intersection) asks for a
single analytic sphere hit. At the center pixel, the ray must hit; at a corner
pixel it must miss. Draw the normal as a color to inspect the intersection
before adding reflection.

**Boundary to remember:** ray tracing here means a small educational fragment
shader, not a promise of production-quality photorealistic rendering.
