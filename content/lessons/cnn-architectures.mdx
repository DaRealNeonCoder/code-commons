The Intro course applied one hand-designed edge filter. A CNN becomes useful when it learns many filters and composes them across depth. Early layers can detect local edges; later layers combine them into corners, textures, parts, and object-level evidence.

## Channels, kernels, and feature maps

An RGB image has three input channels. A convolution kernel has a matching input-channel dimension and produces one output channel. A layer with 32 kernels produces 32 feature maps. Each kernel is reused at every spatial location, which gives CNNs translation-aware local patterns with fewer parameters than a dense layer over every pixel.

## Shape, padding, and stride

For one spatial dimension, a kernel of width `K`, padding `P`, and stride `S` produces:

```text
output_width = floor((input_width + 2P - K) / S) + 1
```

Padding can preserve border information and stride can downsample. Pooling is another downsampling operation, but it has no learned weights. Track shapes after every block; a shape mismatch is usually more informative than a mysterious training failure.

## Receptive fields grow with depth

One `3 × 3` layer sees a `3 × 3` patch. Two stride-one `3 × 3` layers give a theoretical `5 × 5` receptive field, while keeping fewer parameters than one `5 × 5` layer. Stride and pooling enlarge the region seen by deeper features. This is why architecture depth changes what “local” means.
