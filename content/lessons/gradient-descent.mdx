**Gradient descent** is a repeatable way to search for a low value. Imagine standing on a smooth hill in the fog. You can feel which way is uphill, so you take a small step in the opposite direction.

For one number `w`, the update is:

```text
w = w - learning_rate × gradient
```

For many numbers, `w` is a vector and `gradient` is a gradient vector. The rule is the same.

## The learning rate

The **learning rate** controls the step size:

- too small: safe, but many steps;
- just right: moves quickly toward the bottom;
- too large: jumps past the bottom, or can bounce forever.

Try the bowl `f(w) = (w - 4)²`. Its bottom is at `w = 4`. Starting at `0`, a learning rate of `0.25` moves close to the bottom in a few updates.

## Convergence

When the updates become tiny and the function value stops improving, we say the process **converged**. It may not land on the exact answer because we usually stop after a fixed number of steps or when the change is small enough.

```python
for step in range(20):
    w = w - learning_rate * gradient(w)
```

In a real model, the “function value” is called a **loss**. Training tries to make the loss smaller by changing the model's weights.

## Local minima and bumpy surfaces

A smooth bowl has one clear lowest point. A bumpy function can have several low points. Gradient descent may settle in a nearby **local minimum** rather than the absolute lowest point. Different starting points and learning rates can lead to different paths.

The demo above treats the loss as a curve: the ball is the current weight, and each update uses the slope at that point to move downhill. Try a cautious rate and then a jumpy one to compare the paths.

## Tune-the-knob math puzzle

Start at `w = 0` for `f(w) = (w - 4)²`. Which learning rate reaches the lowest value in under 20 steps?

Try these by hand or in the puzzle: `0.01`, `0.25`, `0.5`, and `1.1`. A rate can be too cautious or too jumpy. There is not one magic rate for every surface; tuning is part of using an optimizer.

> [!TIP]
> Gradient descent is not guessing a new answer from scratch. It makes a small correction based on the slope it can see right now.
