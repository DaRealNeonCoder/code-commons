A **bit** is a small piece of information with two possible values: `0` or `1`. We can describe the values in several equivalent ways:

| Bit | Signal level | Everyday meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0` | low | no / false / off |
| `1` | high | yes / true / on |

The bit is an abstraction. Inside a particular device, “low” and “high” are ranges of voltages rather than perfect mathematical points. We use `0` and `1` because the two ranges are easier to distinguish reliably than many different levels.

## Binary place values

Binary is base 2, so each position is a power of 2. From right to left, the places are 1, 2, 4, 8, and so on:

```text
1 0 1 1
8 4 2 1   <- place values
```

The binary number `1011` means `1×8 + 0×4 + 1×2 + 1×1 = 11`. A group of four bits can represent 16 different patterns, from `0000` to `1111`.

## Digital does not mean magical

A digital signal still travels through a physical wire. “Digital” means we agree to interpret a range of physical measurements as one of two logical values. That agreement gives circuits room to tolerate small amounts of noise.
