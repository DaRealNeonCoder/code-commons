# From formulas to shader art

Signed distances combine predictably:

- `min(a, b)` is a union, because either shape can contain the point.
- `max(a, b)` is an intersection when the distances are oriented correctly.
- `max(a, -b)` subtracts one shape from another.

Small hash functions can provide repeatable value noise without a texture. Use
noise as a color or displacement ingredient, not as a replacement for
understanding the base shape. For pointer-driven art, `iMouse.xy` is in
normalized canvas coordinates; map it into the same centered coordinate system
as your SDF.

## Checkpoint and mini-project

The [SDF composition puzzle](/shaders/graphics-sdf-composition) asks for a
ring made from two circles. Its target is checked at a fixed resolution, so a
correct `max(dOuter, -dInner)` beats a hand-tuned image.

**Mini-project — reactive poster:** combine three SDFs, a moving gradient, and
the pointer position to make a poster. Save two versions: one with an
intermediate distance-field visualization and one with your final palette.

This completes the shader-art track's first arc. From here, the course keeps
that workflow available while introducing a second workflow only when meshes
and CPU-side control become useful.
