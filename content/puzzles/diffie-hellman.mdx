The Diffie-Hellman key exchange lets two people, Alice and Bob, agree on a shared secret over a public channel.

They first agree on a prime number `p` and a generator `g`. Each picks a private key greater than 1 and less than `p`, and publishes the matching public key: `g` to the power of the private key, modulo `p`.

Each then combines the other's public key with their own private key: the other's public key to the power of your private key, modulo `p`. Remarkably, both of them arrive at the same shared secret, which an eavesdropper who only saw the public keys cannot easily compute.

Your task is to carry out these two calculations.

## Input

One line with four values: the mode (`public` or `secret`), the prime `p`, and two numbers `a` and `b`. For `public`, `a` is the generator `g` and `b` is a private key; for `secret`, `a` is the other party's public key and `b` is your private key.

## Output

For `public`, print the public key `g^b mod p`. For `secret`, print the shared secret `a^b mod p`.
